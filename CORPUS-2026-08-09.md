# Widening the corpus, 2026-08-09

The corpus went from **84 documents to 232**. The point was not more material for
its own sake: it was to find out whether the headline figures describe the *app*
or the *sample*. [CORPUS-2026-08-08.md](CORPUS-2026-08-08.md) records the last
time that question went unasked — the numbers turned out to describe a sample
that was 65% material this app is not for.

## What changed about the draw

The 84 were sampled from attachments **added to Zotero since 2021-08-08**: a
five-year window, 1,893 candidates of the right item types. That bound was
arbitrary, and it meant everything older in the library — the majority of it —
had never been eligible.

This draw removed the window. Of 14,960 candidate attachments of the right
types, **13,067 had never been sampled from**, going back to 2012-12-12.

```sh
python3 Tools/sample-zotero.py --dest testdocs --per-bucket 6 --seed 11 \
    --exclude-manifest <the previous manifest>
```

Same eight item types, same four eras, same **scanned-only gate**. The gate did
real work: to find 148 new scans it rejected **429 born-digital, 40
photographed, and 4 with no page image at all**. That ratio is the reason the
gate exists.

**148 new documents, 79% of them added before the old window** — 117 predate
2021-08-08, spread across every year from 2013 on. Total: 232 documents, 696
measured pages, 1.2 GB.

Two buckets could not be filled and the sampler said so rather than padding:
`report/new` found 2 scans in 59 candidates, `newspaperArticle/undated` none in
2. Recent reports are almost all born-digital. That is a fact about the library,
not a defect in the draw.

## The result: the figures were not an artifact of the sample

| | original 84 | new 148 | all 232 |
|---|---|---|---|
| processed successfully | 84 / 84 | **148 / 148** | **232 / 232** |
| line-start selectability | median 100%, mean 99.69 | median 100%, mean 99.72 | median 100%, mean 99.71 |
| line-end selectability | median 100%, mean 99.58 | median 100%, mean 99.53 | median 100%, mean 99.55 |
| word retention | median 100%, mean 99.79 | median 100%, mean 99.75 | median 100%, mean 99.76 |
| text-layer offset | median −0.10, max 0.10 | median −0.05, max 0.10 | median −0.10, max 0.10 |
| worst line-end | 91% | 92% | 91% |

Nothing scored below 90% on any measure, in either half. The new material —
older, never previously eligible, drawn with a different seed — behaves
indistinguishably from the material the app's figures were calibrated on.

The worst ten documents on line-end are 1920s–50s newspaper clippings, and they
are drawn from both halves in roughly equal number. That is the failure mode
C18 and C20 both address, and it is a property of dense newsprint rather than of
which documents happened to be sampled.

## One number did move, and it is the material

**Source line tightness went from 1.33% to 2.40%** on the new material (221 of
9,218 adjacent line pairs set closer than their own boxes, against 74 of 5,564
before). Corpus-wide it is now 2.00%.

This is not a regression. D3 established that this column measures the
*document*, not the text layer: it counts how tightly the source was typeset,
and high values mark material that is hard to lay a text layer over. The older
draw simply contains more tightly-set printing — which is what you would expect
from a wider reach back into an archive-heavy library, and it makes the corpus a
harder test than it was.

## What this does and does not license

It licenses the headline figures: they now rest on 232 documents rather than 84,
and on material selected without an arbitrary recency bound.

It does not license calling the app finished. Every adversarial pass over this
codebase has found real defects, including in code written during the previous
pass. A corpus is evidence about the pipeline's output, not about the paths that
never ran.
