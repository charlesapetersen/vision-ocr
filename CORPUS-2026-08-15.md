# Corpus classification, 2026-08-15 — the gate asking the app's own question

The whole 233-document corpus re-classified after `REVIEW-2026-08-14.md` **A12.4**:
`Tools/classify-source.swift` had its own copy of the page-image test, and the corpus
gate whose only purpose is D1 consequently admitted the two documents the shipped app
puts a "this file already has digital text" modal in front of.

**This is a record of one run, not a claim about the present.** The full output is
committed beside it as `CORPUS-2026-08-15.tsv`, 233 rows, so every figure below can be
recomputed without the 1.2 GB of PDFs — which are not committed. `CORPUS-2026-08-08.md`
and `CORPUS-2026-08-09.md` are the earlier records; **their verdicts came from the old
predicate** and are not comparable row by row with these.

**One difference between the committed file and that command's output:** a header row was

## What A12.4's stated mechanism got wrong

A12.4 says the two aggregates "pass a test **no single page passes**": `maxImage` is a
max over five pages while `medianDPI` is a median over those pages, so one page's raster
can combine with another page's DPI. That is structurally true and **it is not what
happened**. Over all 233 documents the minimum `imagePages` is **1** — every document the
old test admitted had at least one page that passes `Flattener.pageIsAnImage` on its own,
so the chimera the entry describes never occurred here.

The mechanism that did produce the disagreement is plainer: a **max** over five pages is
an *any-page* rule wearing an aggregate's clothes, and it was being read as a statement
about the document. The count A12.4 gives — 7 documents disagreeing — reproduces exactly,
against a majority rule.

## Why the document-level rule is *any* page, measured

There is no shipped answer to copy for "is this document a scan": the app asks
`pageIsAnImage` of a page and `hasDigitalText` of a document, and neither is that
question. So the rule is this tool's own, and it was measured rather than picked.

A majority rule (`imagePages * 2 > sampled`, mirroring `hasDigitalText`) rejects **7**:

| document | imagePages/sampled | hasDigitalText | what it is |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `Canby_1929` | 1/4 | **yes** | born-digital — correctly rejected |
| `Davis_2005` | 1/5 | **yes** | born-digital — correctly rejected |
| `bookSection/full chapter.pdf` | 1/5 | no | a ~100 DPI scan, rasters 810–987 px |
| `magazineArticle/Glazer_2002` | 1/2 | no | 2-page scan, p2 an 816 px blank |
| `newspaperArticle/Zipkin_2000` | 1/2 | no | 2-page scan, p2 a 460 px page |
| `newspaperArticle/_1973_Other 67` | 2/4 | no | 4-page clipping, 2 rasters ≥ 900 px |
| `thesis/Schwaller - 2026` | 2/5 | no | genuinely mixed — see below |

`full chapter.pdf` is the instructive one. Its 32 pages are a scan at about 100 DPI, and
the 900 px bar runs straight through them:

```
classify-source's 5 indices   p6  834  p11 810  p17 810  p22 918  p27 858   -> 1 of 5 an image
hasDigitalText's 4 indices    p7  933  p13 987  p20 948  p26 930            -> 4 of 4 an image
```

Two strides through one book, opposite answers, and neither is wrong about the pages it
looked at. A majority rule on a five-page sample of a document sitting on a threshold is
close to a coin toss; the `any` rule is not.

**And the same objection lands on `hasDigitalText`, which this gate makes decisive.** That
rule is *also* a majority over a sample, so preferring it cannot be justified by disliking
majorities-over-samples. The reason to prefer it is narrower and worth stating exactly: it
is **the app's own question**, so the gate agrees with the shipped app by construction, and
a disagreement between them becomes a finding about the app rather than a difference of
opinion between two rules. Where the app is fragile, the gate now inherits that fragility
visibly instead of substituting its own.

**How fragile, measured on the same document.** `full chapter.pdf` has 32 pages, of which
**8** clear the 900 px bar and **23** count as "digital" by the shipped rule (≥120 characters
and not a page image). Re-evaluating `hasDigitalText` over `sampleIndices(32, wanted)`:

```
wanted= 1  1/1   YES        wanted= 7   5/7  YES
wanted= 2  1/2   no         wanted= 8   6/8  YES
wanted= 3  3/3   YES        wanted= 9   5/9  YES
wanted= 4  0/4   no   <- shipped   wanted=10   9/10 YES
wanted= 5  4/5   YES        wanted=11  10/11 YES
wanted= 6  5/6   YES        wanted=12   8/12 YES
```

**The shipped `wanted: 4` is the only value above 2 that answers "no".** So the 230 in the
table above is 230 *at the sample size the app uses*, and would be **229** at almost any
other — the corpus would lose a document that is, on the evidence, a genuine ~100 DPI scan.
`Canby_1929` and `Davis_2005` are stable (born-digital at every sample size 1…12), so the
two documents this record is *about* do not move; the sensitivity is confined to the
borderline case, which is the definition of a borderline case.

The deeper reading is that the fragile term is not the sample size but the **900 px bar**.
23 of 32 pages of a scan count as "digital text" only because a 100 DPI page raster is 810
px across on a Letter sheet. Sampling more pages would make the app *more* wrong about this
document, and correctly more right about `Schwaller` — `REVIEW-2026-08-14.md` A3.5 carries
that finding, and it is the app's to fix, not the gate's.

`Schwaller` is the one genuine mixed document: sampled pages 51 and 201 **draw no image
at all** while carrying 2,900 and 2,783 characters, and pages 101 and 251 are 2,640 px
plates. `BUGS.md` C24 found pages that draw no image at all in five documents — Batzell,
Kelly_2014, AI 2027, Schwaller and Lyons — so it and this table share exactly one member,
and it is this one. (An earlier draft of this paragraph said "the same five documents",
which reads as the five above it and is false: only Schwaller is in both.) Its own
`hasDigitalText` is 2 of 4 — one page short of the app warning about it — so the gate and
the app agree, which is the property worth having even when the answer is arguable.

So: the majority rule rejects **no** non-scan that `hasDigitalText` misses, and costs at
least four scans. The gate is `hasDigitalText` first, then any sampled page being a
page-sized raster. `imagePages` and `sampled` are in the TSV, so a majority or all-pages
rule can be recomputed from this run — an all-pages rule would reject **21**.

## What this does and does not invalidate

- **"233/233 process successfully" stands.** It is a robustness claim about the pipeline
  over 233 real files, and it does not depend on what kind of file each is.
- **"every one of them a scan" does not.** It is 230 of 233 by the current gate, with the
  two born-digital documents named above and one misclassified scan (R55).
- **The text-layer figures** (`start=`, `end=`, `off=`, `words=`) are per-document and
  unaffected; the two born-digital documents are 9 pages, and any median over 16,987 pages
  is unmoved. Their `words=` figures are the D1 measurement problem in miniature — OCR
  reproducing text the file already had — but at 0.05% of the corpus.
- **Nothing was removed from `testdocs/`.** Re-cutting the corpus changes every published
  figure at once; this record exists so that decision can be made with the numbers rather
  than instead of them.
- **230 is 230 at the app's sample size.** One of the 233, `bookSection/full chapter.pdf`,
  sits one sample-size step from being called born-digital by `hasDigitalText` itself — see
  the sensitivity table above. Quote the figure with the gate's version, the way the MRC
  figures are quoted with theirs.
