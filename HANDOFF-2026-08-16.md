## The three things worth carrying forward

**1 · A threshold with no gap is not always a tuned threshold.** R81's population — pairs of
observations that overlap heavily — sits in a continuum, and two attempts to separate it by
choosing a cut point (room over box width; overlap in points) found no gap, which is where C24's
repairs died. What worked was giving up on the cut point and finding the *structural* statement:
two boxes on one line that overlap by more than half of the narrower one are not two fragments,
they are two readings of the same ink. The corpus then does a different job — not choosing the
constant, but showing it is not on a knife edge: of 852 pairs, 779 share at most 0.47 of the
narrower box and 70 share at least 0.53, **three** lie in between, and moving the constant from
0.5 to 0.4 or 0.6 changes the verdict on six of them.

**2 · An instrument that cannot move is worse than no instrument.** Every column of `score-corpus`
and `score-line-separation` reads **identical** across R82 on all eight invariant-3 documents,
while the extracted text gains seventeen spaces. Nothing in `Tools/` could count a weld, which is
most of why A1.1 sat open — and `score-corpus`'s `end=` column is *structurally unable* to see
R81, because over a run drawn at 5% of its box the right-hand probe rectangle is answered by the
line above. Two instruments came out of this session: `Tools/score-run-width` (property (c), and
the ceiling that fights it) and `welded=W/N` in `score-line-separation` (property (d), counted
for the first time).

**3 · The obvious fix for C23 was wrong in two different ways, both measured.** Its own entry
proposed carrying a `/CropBox` through `Flattener.flatten` and into `JBIG2.assemble`. On the
rebuild routes recognition reads **whole-sheet bitmaps**, so a crop box on the intermediate makes
`compose` map every observation into the trimmed rectangle and shifts every run on the page. And
`qpdf --overlay` wraps both layers in form XObjects whose `/BBox` is the destination's **crop**
box and centres that box on the media box — measured, the page image came out translated by
(50, 96) with everything outside the crop clipped away for good, which is invariant 1's harm
arriving through the fix for C23.

## What C23 cost, and how that cost was removed the same day

**The first fix for C23 gave up the JBIG2 route for trimmed documents** — 16 of 233 corpus
documents, 627 of 16,987 pages, measured at **2.26x the bytes** on `Canby_1929`. That went into
the register as a considered refusal: "there is nowhere else to put the crop", since it cannot be
present when `qpdf --overlay` runs and neither CGPDFContext nor PDFKit can rewrite those pages
afterwards without dropping `/JBIG2Decode` (measured, 7,391 → 13,401 bytes, compression gone).

**The refusal was wrong, and cheaply so.** It named `fix-qdf` as the only route and rejected it
on cost. `qpdf --update-from-json` — one more invocation, no second binary, no hand-authored PDF
syntax — patches a page dictionary and leaves every stream alone. "There is nowhere else to put
it" was a claim about qpdf's documentation made without reading qpdf's documentation to the end.

The crop box now goes on **after** the merge, in `JBIG2.setCropBoxes`:

- `Canby_1929`, same four pages: **91,595 bytes with compression** against 206,002 on the
  fallback — the crop box costs **477 bytes instead of 114,884**.
- All 16 trimmed documents re-verified: **42 pages, 0 differing**.
- Only a qpdf older than 11 takes the Flate route now, through `wantsJBIG2`'s
  `cropWouldBeLost:` term.

### The trap in it, and this is the part to read before touching it

**`--update-from-json` replaces an object; it does not merge into one.** A patch carrying only
`/CropBox` produced a page with *only* a crop box: **7,391 bytes became 391**, `/Contents` and
the image gone — and `qpdf --check` called the file healthy. Page count cannot see it either, so
neither can `publishVerified`.

`setCropBoxes` is built around that: the page dictionary is **read back from qpdf's own
serialisation and handed straight back with one key added** — the function never authors one —
and the result is verified by comparing every page's content and image object lists against the
file going in. The suite drives the whole path and checks the **ink**, not the page count.

## The research, and the three things it decided

`RESEARCH-2026-08-16.md` is the new file. It exists because the owner asked the right question —
*are we re-deriving things other people have already solved?* — after four refused routing
rounds. Read it before designing anything in this area. The short version:

**1 · `reserveEms = 0.25` is now bounded rather than calibrated.** Every extractor re-derives
word breaks from the drawn gap, and three of the four are readable: poppler needs 0.10 em,
pdf.js 0.102, mupdf 0.15 — and mupdf emits *two* spaces above 0.30. **The window is
(0.15, 0.30) em** and our value sits in the middle of it. That is written on the constant now.

**2 · The hypothesis that would have retired the whole weld class is refuted.** Per-word runs
with real space characters do *not* make word breaks safe: poppler consumes a real `U+0020` as a
word terminator and then **discards** it, re-deciding from geometry; pdf.js drops whitespace
before its heuristic runs. Tesseract has emitted real spaces since 2019 and OCRmyPDF still
documents its layer running words together in macOS Preview. Both projects settled on the same
compromise we already had — hold the gap open with a constant — minus the derivation.

Also worth knowing: **our own 64% figure, the one that justified per-line runs, is confounded.**
It measured another tool's per-word output *with no spaces*, which is the pre-2019 Tesseract
design. Re-run it before citing it again.

**3 · There is one real architectural finding, and it is parked with its costs.** Tesseract has
**two knobs where we have one** — the font size owns the vertical extent, `Tz` owns the width
fit — so it needs no vertical squash at all. Ours couples them, and the squash exists only to
undo that coupling; that is the mechanism behind "four properties that fight each other". We
cannot adopt it piecemeal: stretching a *real* font horizontally scales its side bearings and
splits words, which is what our own `draw` comment already recorded. Tesseract escapes it by
using a **glyphless fixed-pitch** font. The package is costed in `FEATURES.md`; the prerequisite
is one measurement nobody upstream has ever run, against PDFKit, which we can do.

  `mutate.py`'s mutant SURVIVED while eleven green checks said nothing about the shipped code.
  Read the constant before anything writes it. (CONTRIBUTING 4a earns its place again.)

  one of them being unnecessary — the property has its own check now, over a fixture the other
  guard deliberately lets through.

  measured with an unbounded ceiling against an "after" measured with the real one. Both columns
  now come from one binary, with "before" produced by putting the two constants back.
- **A test that tested C13 twice.** The first C23 block asked for a Flate rebuild of an
  image-only page with JBIG2 off — `willRebuild` is `(rebuild && hasEmbeddedText) || wantsJBIG2`,
  so nothing rebuilt and the block re-tested the non-rebuild path. It passed.
- **A second `coordinate` formatter three lines from the first**, in the same file. C20's shape
  at its smallest, and it lasted about four minutes.
- **A refusal written without reading the tool's documentation.** C23 shipped with the JBIG2

  possible alternative, priced it, and refused it — and never checked whether qpdf had a better

  `pgrep -f mutate.py` also matches the `until ! pgrep -f mutate.py` loop waiting for it, which
  is the self-matching trap already recorded for `pgrep -f build/tests`. Wait on the process
  with a pattern that cannot match your own waiter.
