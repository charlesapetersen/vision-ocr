## 2. Models found by the survey (queued as ocr-lab-round2)

- **LightOnOCR-2**: no LightOnOCR-3 and nothing above 1B exists. 1540 px is its TRAINING resolution
  (config `image_size` and processor `longest_edge` both 1540), so `--max-side 1540` is correct. No prompt;
  generation_config temperature 0.2, top_p 0.9; allow 4096 tokens. olmOCR-Bench (paper): old scans 42.2 for the
  tested RL build, 45.4 for `-ocr-soup`, 47.0 for `-base`; tiny text 91.4 / 90.3 / 89.1. The RL step that raised
  the overall score lowered old scans by about 5. `-ocr-soup` and `-base` have no MLX build; mlx-vlm converts them.
- **Churro 3B** (`stanford-oval/churro-3B`, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen research licence): CHURRO-DS printed/handwritten
  NLS 82.3/70.1 on an in-distribution test split. GGUF: `mradermacher/churro-3B-GGUF`. **A ready MLX 8-bit port
  exists: `kintopp/churro-mlx` (about 4.3 GB).** Prompt is a system message only; output is XML (take text as
  the repo's `extract_actual_text_from_xml`, strip tags on a parse failure); diplomatic transcription.
- **Infinity-Parser2-Flash** (2B, Qwen3.5, Apache-2.0; vendor olmOCR-Bench 86.0); **Falcon-OCR** (0.27B, native
  in mlx-vlm); **olmOCR-2-7B** (modernises spelling, collapses on whole multi-column pages); **Qwen3-VL-8B/4B**.
- **For newspapers the cut matters more than the reader** (NewsBench, 15 LoC pages, one author:
  github.com/nealcaren/newsbench): DocLayout-YOLO regions read by GLM-OCR or PaddleOCR-VL 0.970, against at
  most 0.82 for any model reading the whole page. Noted in `ocr-hybrid-proto`.

## 3. What else exists (prior art)

Nothing combines a free native Mac app for non-technical users, an on-device model suited to old print and
newspapers, word-placed text under the scan with column order and copy/Find behaviour, and compact output.
Closest: **OwlOCR 7** (paid, closed Mac app with an undisclosed downloadable "OCR AI" model; its AI mode returns
plain text and a separate Vision pass positions it); **local-llm-pdf-ocr** (§4); **GLM-OCR-Studio** and **Local
OCR** (native Mac MLX apps, September 2026, no users yet, no historical focus); **OCRmyPDF + the AppleOCR
plugin** (same engine as today's Vision tier, CLI); Transkribus and ArchiveLM (historical, cloud, paid);
OCR4all, eScriptorium, Kraken (historical, server, no look-alike PDF); ABBYY FineReader ($69/yr, no VLM).
Free Apple-Vision searchable-PDF tools are common (mac-ocr, PDFOCR, LocalOCR, PDFify, PDF Expert), so the
Vision tier's distinction is its text-layer quality, not the feature. No project found tests its text layer's
copy/Find behaviour or offers an engine choice per document.

## 4. local-llm-pdf-ocr (github.com/ahnafnafee/local-llm-pdf-ocr, MIT)

Python: Surya detection (boxes only), one whole-page VLM call (olmOCR-2 by default, through LM Studio/Ollama),
a dynamic-programming alignment of the model's lines onto the boxes, re-reads of empty boxes from crops, then an
invisible Helvetica layer over a re-rendered JPEG of the page.

**Worth borrowing** — it maps onto `ocr-hybrid-proto` arrangement (b), and would let (b) read a page in ONE
model call instead of one per line (a newspaper has 800+ lines), which bears directly on the 3× speed bar:

1. **Monotonic alignment of the model's lines onto the detected lines** (`src/.../aligner.py:362-458`). Each box's
   expected characters = its area share × the page's total characters. Match cost is asymmetric: overfill
   (actual−expected)/actual, underfill (expected−actual)/expected × 0.5 (`:322-359`); skip a line 1.0, skip a box
   0.4 (`:304-305`). A skipped line joins the last matched box, so no text is lost. The asymmetry fixed a
   one-box shift cascade that duplicated text (commit 9a9e002). Here the box order would be this app's own
   column order (`prepared`/`columnOrdered`), which is better than their two fixed orders.
2. **Order check by alignment cost**: they align under row-major and column-major box orders and keep the
   cheaper (`aligner.py:143-163`). Here: a high cost under this app's order says the model read the columns
   differently — a signal to fall back, not to reorder.
3. **Alignment confidence → fallback**: a page with ≥8 boxes and real matches for under half of them is redone
   per box (`pipeline.py:42-43, 320-338`); it took their form page from text similarity 0.70 to 0.96. Here the
   fallback is per-line crops (arrangement b) or Vision's own text (c). Degenerate alignments (no matches, or one
   line against ≥5 boxes) must never be stuffed into one box: here, keep Vision's line.
4. **Crop guards**: skip a crop whose grey standard deviation is under 12 (a blank crop makes a model invent
   text; `utils/image.py:36-65, 111`); drop the model's stock pangram/lorem output; cap a line repeated more than
   20 times; per-call token and time caps.
5. **Dense straight print only: paint neighbouring boxes white in a line's crop** (boxes ≥45% inside the target
   stay); never on handwriting, where it cost 0.21 (`pipeline.py:673-722`). Relevant to newspapers (C41).
6. **Containment-only dedup** of overlapping boxes (≥30% overlap, both ≥8 characters, keep the superset; no fuzzy
   matching) — took a case from bag-of-words F1 0.784 to 0.972. Compare with this app's band merging.
7. **Perspective-warp a tilted line's quad flat before reading it**; an axis-aligned crop of a slanted line pulls
   in its neighbours (`image.py:216-312`).

**Do NOT borrow**:

- **Their text placement.** They stretch each line horizontally to 98% of its box (`core/pdf.py:276-358`). This
  app tried stretching and rejected it because extraction then splits words; its fit-by-size placement and
  invariant 3 are better tested than theirs.
- **Their page images.** They re-render every page as JPEG (q80), losing the original image. They also detect,
  read and crop from one 1024 px JPEG at q50 (`core/pdf.py:69-76`), which is too coarse for small print.
- **Their unfiltered model output.** They leave olmOCR's Markdown, HTML and LaTeX in the text they align, so
  tags land in the layer.
- **Their all-or-nothing failure.** Grounded mode aborts the whole document on one empty page. Degrade per page
  instead.
- **Their column split.** They split columns at one global gap in box centres, so a full-width headline lands on
  whichever side its centre falls.

Their evaluation is thin: 15 history rows, two fixtures bootstrapped from the pipeline's own output. What
transfers is the algorithm, not evidence that it works here; measure it on the truth set like any arrangement.
