# Whole-codebase review, 2026-08-14

A sequential adversarial review of the codebase, one area at a time, at the owner's
request. **This file is the durable record of what was found, not a summary of what was
fixed** — each finding says whether it was verified by running code or only reasoned
about, and nothing here has been fixed unless the entry says so.

Findings that get fixed graduate into `BUGS.md` with an R-number and are struck through
here. Findings that are refused stay here with the reason.

Read `CONTRIBUTING.md` §3 before acting on any of these: **the instrument is wrong more
often than the code**, and this project has a long history of "confirmed" findings that
were measurement artefacts. Every entry below that is marked VERIFIED was verified by the
reviewer running code; that is not the same as being independently reproduced, and where a
finding proposes a constant change, the four invariant-3 probes have **not** been re-run.

## Area 1 — `Sources/SearchableWriter.swift` (the text layer)

ARCHITECTURE.md names this risk #1: four properties must hold at once and each has
historically been broken by a fix to another. Two real defects, both measured on real
corpus pages.

### A1.1 · Words weld again — property (d) fails in a measured band. VERIFIED, OPEN

**31 welded word pairs across 21 real newspaper pages**, read back out of the composed
file with PDFKit. `ity`+`companies`, `female`+`member`, `Greece`+`(AP)_The`. This is C18's
symptom — `valuablestudy` — returned for a class of cases.

**Mechanism.** `reserveEms` (the constant that holds property (d) open) can only act
through `rightLimit`, and `rightLimit` only considers a neighbour that `isSameVisualLine`
accepts: baselines within `sameLineBaselineFraction` (0.4) × **min**(a.height, b.height).
The `min` is deliberate — C20 chose it so a tall display numeral cannot claim a body line
two rows away — but it means a pair where one fragment is short gets a tolerance of only
1.4–2.1 pt, while **PDFKit's own line grouping is far looser (~8 pt)**. In the band
between the two, no reserve is opened, the run is drawn to its full box width, and the
words weld. `headroom` does not cover it either: side-by-side fragments have no horizontal
overlap, so its test skips them.

Synthetic boundary, verified: two 9 pt fragments, gap ≤ 2 pt, baseline offset **3.7 pt**
against a tolerance of 3.6 → `the valuablestudy of it`. At 3.5 pt → correct. Setting
`sameLineBaselineFraction = 1.0` fixes every welded case in the sweep, which identifies
the gate as the cause.

**Do not simply raise the constant.** It is shared with `headroom` on purpose (C20 unified
them), and loosening it globally makes `headroom` skip real vertical neighbours — which is
the overlap defect C20 existed to fix. The reserve direction needs its own, looser
predicate, or `max` height for that direction only. **All four invariant-3 probes must be
re-run**, and nothing in the suite currently covers this band: `mutate.py` kills
`const/sameLineBaselineFraction` only with a "the calibrated value is 0.4" pin.

`Sources/SearchableWriter.swift:1006`, with `:929` and `:947-952`.

### A1.2 · A whole line can be drawn at 5% of its width, silently. VERIFIED, OPEN

`rightLimit` only accepts a neighbour whose left edge is right of this run's, so
`allowed > 0` **unconditionally** — which means the guard the comment at `:1013-1014`
claims ("`allowed > 0` already refuses a heavy overlap") cannot fire. When Vision emits a
same-line fragment starting a few points right of a wide fragment's left edge, `allowed`
is a couple of points, the font size hits its 0.5 pt floor, and the line is drawn as a
~1.5 pt sliver. No `Unplaced` is reported.

Verified on `testdocs/newspaperArticle/___ 3.pdf` p6: a 264 pt-wide, 60-character line
drawn **13.36 pt wide (5% of the ink)** and 1.5 pt tall, because its nearest same-line
neighbour starts 3.62 pt to its right. Probing the output: the leftmost 6 pt selects the
whole line, the right half selects *other lines'* text, the bottom 3 pt selects nothing —
the line is effectively unhighlightable. Rate over 21 newspaper pages: **28 of 6,997
fragments below 15% of their box width, 62 below 50%**, several at 0.0%.

Characters are still in the content stream, so *search* is unaffected; property (c) is
silently lost. The fix is to make the comment's intent real — ignore a "neighbour" that
leaves this run no usable width — rather than clamping toward zero.

`Sources/SearchableWriter.swift:1102`.

## Area 2 — `Sources/Model.swift` (pipeline, publish, cancellation) and `Sources/JBIG2.swift`

`JBIG2.assemble` came out of this **sound** — see the checked list. Everything below is in
`Model.swift`, and two of the three are about publishing the wrong thing.

### ~~A2.1 · `publish` cannot replace an existing file on another volume~~ — FIXED, `BUGS.md` R59

`publish` uses `replaceItemAt(output, withItemAt: staged)` when the destination exists, and
`staged` is always in `NSTemporaryDirectory()` — the boot volume. `replaceItemAt` requires
both items on **one** volume and returns POSIX 18 `EXDEV` otherwise. The `else` branch
(`moveItem`) is fine, because Foundation copies across devices. So **the first run to an
external drive or network share succeeds and every later run over the same output fails**:

```
pass 1: succeeded   901054 bytes, 3 pages
pass 2: failed      "The file “Stiglitz.ocr.pdf” couldn’t be saved in the folder “pubtest”."
```

Verified against the real `makeSearchablePDF` with the output on a mounted volume, on both
HFS+ and APFS. Trigger: output folder on any non-boot volume — or *Save beside each
original* with the originals there — plus an output already present. Correct a setting,
press Start again, and every file in the batch fails with a message that names neither the
cause nor a remedy. Invariant 2 holds (the old file survives; nothing partial is written),
so this is total failure rather than content loss. The same applies to *Use Existing Text*;
plain Extract Text escapes it because it writes atomically to the target.

`Sources/Model.swift:1302-1309`.

### A2.2 · Cancel is observed and the file is published anyway. VERIFIED — searchable path FIXED (R59), text path STILL OPEN

The last cancellation check is at `:1735`; `publish` is at `:1821`. Everything between is
uninterruptible: two `PDFPageCount` reads, the unplaced check, `copyOutline` (a full PDFKit
re-serialisation) and — when *Keep highlights and notes* is on — the transplant's three
qpdf passes. Cancelling in that window publishes the file and reports **`.succeeded`**:

```
cancel fired at: staged.pdf complete (19 pages)
isCancelled: true    outcome: succeeded    published: true, 19 pages
```

Measured window: 0.38 s at 19 pages, 0.73 s at 57 — about 13 ms/page for `copyOutline`
alone, so ≈7 s on a 568-page book, and longer with the transplant. ARCHITECTURE's "three
gates, then publish" and "failure is reported, never published" are both false here. The
existing cancel test only cancels *before* the run starts, so this window is untested.

**The sibling is worse.** `Recogniser.extract` checks `isCancelled` only at the top of each
page, so a cancel during the last page finishes it and writes **straight to the user's
destination with no staging** — and `:1244` then reports `.cancelled`. The log says
"⊘ cancelled" while the previous run's `.txt` has already been replaced. For the
single-page inputs that mode usually gets, that is the whole run.

`Sources/Model.swift:1735 → 1821`, and `:1241-1245`.

### A3.1 · The MRC memory accounting is wrong — but my figures were an instrument artifact. PARTLY REFUTED by verification, OPEN in a narrower form

> **Read this first.** An adversarial verifier attacked this finding and was right to. The
> **omission is real** and `measuredMRCBytesPerPixel = 8.0` **is wrong**, but the 26.5 and
> 30.5 B/px figures below are a **memory-retention artifact**, and the conclusion drawn
> from them was inverted.
>
> **Why the numbers were wrong.** `ru_maxrss` is a high-water mark, and libmalloc keeps
> freed large blocks mapped *and dirty* — so over a multi-phase function it reads as a **sum
> of distinct-size peaks rather than a maximum**. The verifier's control settles it: two
> 8 B/px phases with a `free` between them read **16.38 B/px** by `ru_maxrss` and **8.00**
> by live bytes (`malloc_zone_statistics`). Calling `mrcLayers` twice on one page reads
> 40.23 B/px for a call that demands 18.
>
> **And the phases do not overlap**, which was the crux: `sauvolaMask` returns *before*
> `textRegionMask` and `inverse` exist, so the integral images are never alive with them.
> Measured on one real page: `renderGrey + sauvolaMask` live peak **1,181.8 MB**; the whole
> grey `mrcLayers` live peak **1,181.8 MB** — identical to the byte.
>
> **What survives.** `sauvolaMask`'s two `(w+1)(h+1)` `[Double]` integral images (16 B/px)
> are genuinely absent from the enumeration, and they are the peak: 1 + 8 + 8 + 1 = **18.005
> B/px** analytically against **19.11** measured live. The doc's "about 8 bytes a pixel" is
> an accurate description of the `fillHoles` phase — it simply is not the maximum. So the
> constant is wrong by ~2.3x, not 3.3x.
>
> **The emphasis was inverted.** Matched instruments on the corpus's largest page (64.84 MP):
> the **grey** assertion holds on 3 of 4 instruments; the **colour** one genuinely fails, and
> worst at `PhotoDetail.maximum` (42.4 B/px by peak footprint). Colour is the finding.
>
> **The hazard is slow, not fatal.** 2.6 GB live / 4.2 GB footprint at 100 MP on an 18 GB
> machine, every run completing, `time -l` reporting **0 swaps**. R24's "a failed allocation
> is a crash" does not apply at these sizes.
>
> **`measuredGreyBytesPerPixel = 5.5` does not reproduce either** — `flatten(.grayscale)` on
> the page it came from reads 3.11 live / 7.90 resident / 7.52 footprint. So the "2.20 GB
> render ceiling" both assertions are compared against is really ~3.0 GB, and **the two sides
> of the comparison are in different units**. That is the actual defect: pick peak
> `phys_footprint` and re-measure both.
>
> **Corpus impact: none.** 233 documents, 16,987 pages, **max 64.84 MP, zero pages over
> 72 MP**. Correcting the bound down to 83, 72 or even 70 MP would affect no document.
> *(The verifier also caught that `$(ls testdocs/*/*.pdf)` splits on the spaces in these
> filenames — its first census silently read 13 of 233 documents. Same trap that cost me a
> filename earlier in this sweep.)*
>
> **Reachability is real but unexercised**: in `.grayscale` mode `useBilevel` is false for
> every page, so every page with words reaches `mrcLayers` — not only `isPicture` ones — and
> both gates use `<= 100 MP` *inclusive*.
>
> **What to do, in the verifier's order:** fix the doc (the peak is the max of the phases,
> not their sum); define the unit and re-measure both sides in it; give the **colour** route
> its own lower bound (≈88 MP, or ≈71 at `PhotoDetail.maximum`); make the assertions bite
> with a `--probe-mrc-memory` child that measures a fixture rather than comparing recorded
> constants; and if the memory itself is the target, `sauvolaMask` can use a rolling band of
> `2r+1` rows instead of two full-page integral images — 16 B/px → ~0.2, taking the grey
> route below the doc's *current* claim. `[Float]` is not a safe shortcut: the squared sums
> reach 4.2e12.
>
> Also stale nearby, `Flattener.swift:168-171`: "the largest page in the 78-document corpus
> (21.5 MP)" and "all 4,992 corpus pages". Now 233 documents / 16,987 pages / 64.84 MP; the
> substantive claim ("none exceed 100 MP") still holds.

**The original finding, as reported, follows. Its measurements are the artifact described
above; kept because the omission it found is real.**


`maximumMRCPageMegapixels`'s doc enumerates what layering holds at peak — "the grey buffer,
the stencil, the text-region map, the filled background, and inside `fillHoles` a second
copy of the buffer plus two more flag arrays — about 8 bytes a pixel". **`sauvolaMask` is
not in that list**, and it is the largest thing in the function: two `[Double]` integral
images of `(w+1)×(h+1)`, i.e. **16 bytes a pixel**, allocated two lines after the render.

Measured peak RSS, one page size per process, linear across three sizes:

| | measured | stated |
|---|---|---|
| `sauvolaMask` alone | **17.0 B/px** (8 + 8 + 1, exactly) | not accounted |
| `mrcLayers` grey | **26.5 B/px** | 8.0 |
| `mrcLayers` colour | **30.5 B/px** | 22.0 |

The instrument was checked first (§3): the same harness reads **5.5 B/px** for `flatten` in
`.grayscale`, matching the recorded `measuredGreyBytesPerPixel = 5.5` exactly — so these
numbers are comparable to the ones the constants were set from.

At the shipped bound that is **2.65 GB** grey and **3.05 GB** colour against a render
ceiling of 2.20 GB, so "layering's worst case stays inside the render's" is **false by 1.2x
and 1.4x** — while `mrcBoundIsWithinTheRenderOne` and `colourMRCBoundIsWithinTheRenderOne`
both pass, and the colour one sits exactly on the boundary (2200 ≤ 2200), which is the
signature of a constant tuned to pass rather than measured. R24's recorded harm is that a
failed allocation is a crash that takes every concurrent file with it.

`Tools/mutate.py` mutates `maximumMRCPageMegapixels`, so that mutant is killed by a check
whose input is wrong.

## Area 4 — security and privacy (cross-cutting)

Threat model stated up front, because it decides severity: a local, unsandboxed,
ad-hoc-signed, single-user app processing the user's own documents. Most of what follows is
"write the boundary down", and two things are real.

### A4.1 · The run report contains excerpts of the user's document text. VERIFIED, OPEN

`Model.swift:1789-1798` builds the unplaced-lines failure message out of the recognised
text itself — `p\(page) "\(text.prefix(24))"`, up to three lines — and that message reaches
`finish` → `log` → `RunReport.text`, which copies the log **verbatim** into
`~/Library/Logs/VisionOCR/Run <ts>.txt`. So up to **72 characters of the document's own
text, with page numbers**, land in a file whose own docstring says it is "a file that gets
mailed to whoever is helping you". The report is **on by default**. The same string is also
spoken aloud.

Highest severity-per-line-changed in the whole sweep: keep the count, the page numbers and
the reason, drop the text.

The rest of the report is defensible but should be written down: absolute paths of every
failed input, absolute output path of every success, every filename in the batch, the
destination folder, and the custom-words list verbatim — plus qpdf's stderr, which prefixes
diagnostics with the input filename. A user pasting a report into a bug report leaks their
username, directory layout and every document name. **The password is correctly excluded**
(verified absent from generated text). No rotation or cap — one file per batch forever.

### A4.2 · The updater never validates the update URL's scheme or host. VERIFIED, OPEN

`Updater.parse` accepts any `URL(string:)` for `html_url`, and `ContentView` hands it
straight to `NSWorkspace.shared.open`. Verified against the real parser:
`file:///Applications/Calculator.app` accepted, `x-fake-handler://run?cmd=…` accepted,
`https://not-github.example/evil` accepted. So whoever controls the response body can make
the Download button open an arbitrary local file or any registered scheme handler.

Requires controlling the HTTPS response — ATS is in force with no exceptions, so no plain
MITM — and the marginal gain over publishing a malicious release page is the `file://` and
custom-scheme cases. One guard fixes it: require `https`, optionally pin the host.
`release.notes` is parsed, unbounded (200,000 chars accepted) and **never displayed**.

Otherwise the updater is genuinely careful: one endpoint, one `URLSession` symbol in the
whole binary, cookies off, `User-Agent` and `Accept-Language` pinned to constants, no
identifiers, 15 s timeout, off switch.

### A4.3 · The password's boundary, and a stale plaintext copy the reset cannot reach. VERIFIED

Stored in `UserDefaults` in plain text; recoverable byte-for-byte from the 0600 preferences
plist. **Defensible for this app** — the user's own password to their own file, on a
single-user Mac — and the Keychain would add a prompt-and-entitlement problem to an
ad-hoc-signed app for little gain. What should be written down is the real boundary: any
process running as the user reads it with `defaults read`, no prompt and no TCC gate, and
it is in Time Machine backups and APFS local snapshots.

The actual defect: **`migrateFromPreviousName` copies every key including `password` out of
`com.cp1.VisionReaderGUI` and never removes the source**, while `resetAll` clears only the
current domain. Anyone who set a password before the rename has a stale plaintext copy that
"Reset to Defaults" cannot reach. (On this machine the old domain exists but happens to
hold no password.)

Nit: `Annotations.swift:689-693` writes the `pw` file 0644 and chmods to 0600 afterwards.
Harmless inside a 0700 temp root; creating it with the mode set is free.

### A4.4 · Independent confirmation of A3.4's `jpegData`, and a worse framing

Confirmed independently: `jpegData` has no `grey.count` precondition where both siblings do.
The framing matters more than the finding — a short buffer means **adjacent heap bytes get
JPEG-encoded into the published page image**, so it is memory disclosure into the output
document rather than only a trap. Not reachable from today's callers (all three pass
exactly-sized buffers, traced), and `jpegData` is `static` and already called from `Tools/`.

### A4.5 · Temp files: the permissions answer is good news; the residue is crash-only

**`NSTemporaryDirectory()` is 0700 and owned by the user, and there is no hard-coded `/tmp`
anywhere** (zero hits). So nothing is world-readable in any meaningful sense. All three
scratch roots are per-run UUID directories with `defer` removal, so failure *and*
cancellation are covered.

What survives a SIGKILL: the page bitmaps, the extracted text, the Vision observation JSON,
and — for an **encrypted** source — qpdf's stream dump, i.e. the document's content
*decrypted*, in a file named after the document. Plus the plaintext `pw` file, which is
unlinked only by the directory removal. Exposure is to this user's future processes and to
backups, not to other people. Two cheap improvements: unlink `pw` at last use, and move
`Recogniser.swift:689-694`'s `defer` **above** its `createDirectory` — as written a throw
leaks the directory, and the other two roots get this right.

### A4.6 · `publish`'s rollback is `try?`, and can orphan a hidden copy of the document

**This is my own code from R59.** If the same-volume `replaceItemAt` fails, the rollback
`try? fm.moveItem(at: sibling, to: staged)` can itself fail silently, leaving a complete
copy of the finished document as an invisible, extension-less `.visionocr-publish-<UUID>`
file beside the user's output — same directory, same 0644, so no new audience, but invisible
and it would sync to Dropbox or iCloud unnoticed. Make the rollback failure loud.

### Checked and found sound (area 4)

**Subprocess execution is clean.** No shell string anywhere — every invocation sets an argv
array, verified inert against `"en-US, ; rm -rf ~ , $(whoami), \`id\`"`. **A planted `qpdf`
cannot win**: `locateTool` tries the bundled copy first, and the bundled tools are absolute
paths inside the bundle with `@loader_path` linkage; `containsNativeSlice` rejects a
wrong-architecture copy. The login-shell fallback *is* live and a dotfile does control it —
verified with a planted script — but it is fourth in line, after the bundle and the three
standard prefixes, so it only decides anything when the bundled tool is absent (the Intel
case). Inside the trust boundary of an app the user launched; worth a sentence.

**Untrusted PDF handling: nowhere does the code read a length or offset from the file and
use it to size or index a buffer.** `JBIG2.assemble` takes every dimension from the app's
own render, `/Length` is always `bytes.count`, and outline titles cannot inject PDF syntax.
Recursion over untrusted structure is bounded everywhere (outline depth 32 plus a budget,
`/Kids` cycle guard, annotation-copy cycle safety, Form XObject visited-set). Allocation is
bounded before it happens.

**Nothing phones home but the updater** (one endpoint, one `URLSession` symbol). **No
`os_log`, `NSLog` or `print` in the app at all**, so nothing reaches the unified system log.
No `xattr` or quarantine manipulation (zero hits). Ad-hoc signed with no entitlements and no
hardened runtime — consistent with the documented unnotarized-by-choice decision, and worth
writing down beside "unsandboxed, so it writes wherever you point it".

Residual argv exposure after the password fix: **`--custom-words` and `--languages` go to
the recognition helper in argv**, visible in `ps` while it runs. Custom words are typically
proper nouns lifted from the document being scanned. Very low, but named precisely because
the password was moved out of argv for this reason.

## Area 5 — the batch lifecycle as a state×door table (`Model.swift`, second lens)

CONTRIBUTING §4d's method, applied: 11 states × 10 doors, each cell exercised or marked
untested. **The door the existing table does not have** is S10 × Retry — `Tests/main.swift`
asserts that four doors cannot change a *committed* batch, and says nothing about what
`retryFailures` does when the batch is **not** committed, which is its only real state.

### A5.1 · Retry Failed publishes over a file the same batch deliberately kept off. VERIFIED — CONTENT DESTRUCTION

**The most severe finding in this sweep.** `uniqueOutputs` seeds `claimed` with the batch's
own inputs, precisely so that re-running a folder containing a previous run's results cannot
claim a name another worker is reading. `retryFailures` narrows `files` to the failures, and
`run` re-resolves outputs from that narrowed list — **so the sibling that was doing the
protecting is gone, and the retry claims the name that was reserved away from it.**

Sequence A, run end to end:

```
dir/scan.ocr.pdf   13,006 bytes, text "PRECIOUS ORIGINAL from the previous run"
dir/scan.pdf       arrives truncated
drop dir, save beside each original, Start
  scan.ocr.pdf -> dir/scan.ocr.ocr.pdf      OK
  scan.pdf                                  FAILS, unreadable
dir/scan.ocr.pdf still says PRECIOUS        <- uniqueOutputs did its job
replace dir/scan.pdf with a readable scan, press Retry Failed
  scan.pdf -> dir/scan.ocr.pdf              OK
dir/scan.ocr.pdf   7,268 bytes, "REPLACEMENT SCAN"   <- the user's file is gone
```

Sequence B is the ordinary one-output-folder configuration: two inputs sharing a base name
in different folders, the second fails, and the retry overwrites the first's published
output. Run 1's log **and its run report** both name that path as the first input's result;
the retry's log says nothing, and `renamedOutputs` is empty on a retry so even the "(renamed;
another input claimed that name)" note is absent.

Any per-file failure that does not recur triggers it: a truncated download, an encrypted PDF
once the password is entered, a full or unmounted destination — **or A2.1's `EXDEV`**, which
means the bug I fixed this morning was a trigger for this one. `publish` is atomic; it is
atomic onto the wrong file. Invariant 1 and the spirit of invariant 2 both fail.

Fix direction: seed `claimed` from the retried attempt's `resolvedOutputs` as well as from
the batch's inputs, or have `retryFailures` carry forward the paths it already resolved.
§4b: anything that runs a *subset* of a previous batch has this shape.

### A5.2 · `retryFailures`' put-back never runs, and erases the record of what failed. VERIFIED

`retryFailures` restores the whole batch when `start` declines, and its comment says why —
"reasoning about which refusals are possible is how U21 happened". But the restore is gated
on `guard isCommitted`, and under the shipped defaults `start()` returns with
`isPreflighting == true`, so **the guard always passes and the restore never runs.** Every
refusal arriving after the pre-flight leaves the model gutted:

```
Retry Failed -> pre-flight finds born-digital -> alert -> Cancel
log:      "Start cancelled — nothing was changed."     <- false
files:    [born.pdf]        scan.pdf is gone from the list
outcomes: 0                 every verdict erased
canRetryFailures: false     the record of what failed is unrecoverable
```

### A5.3 · `isImporting` is a boolean, not a count — U21's guard defeated by a second drop. VERIFIED

It is set true by every `add` and false by every completion, so the **first** completion
clears it while other walks are in flight. Two drops, one large:

```
at the moment the small import lands: isImporting = false, canStart = true
Start -> batch total = 1
the 8,000-file walk completes -> REFUSED, "a run is in progress"
```

With a fast batch the walk instead lands *after* `finishUp`: **8,001 rows over "Done — 1 of 1
succeeded"** — U1 verbatim, for the fourth time. Also `start()` never checks `isImporting` at
all, so the interlock exists only in `canStart` and only for the button, which is the
U19/U23 lesson (a property enforced in the view closes one door). `clearFiles()` is not
interlocked either: measured 0 → 8,000 files after a Clear the user explicitly asked for.

### A5.4 · Smaller, from the same lens

- `cancel()` is unguarded and is a **silent no-op** in the committed-but-not-running states —
  `control` does not exist until `run()`, and the "Cancelling…" line it appends is then erased
  by `run()`'s `log = []`. Not reachable from today's UI because the button is rendered only
  `if model.isRunning`, which is one `if` in a view standing between this and a lie.
- **The quit gate and the model disagree about when a batch exists**: `App.swift` gates on
  `RunControl.isAnyRunning`, which is false for the entire committed-but-not-running window,
  so closing the window during "Checking…" quits with a batch the user has pressed Start on.
  Lost work rather than lost content. C20's two-definitions shape.
- In Extract Text with `json`/`jsonl`, the digital-text alert appears carrying text about
  "rebuilding the pages as images discards that text" — nothing is rebuilt in that mode. A
  message naming harm that cannot happen, which pushes the user toward Cancel. A3.3's shape.
- `skipThem` assigns `skipped` where `run` is careful to `subtract`, discarding earlier marks.

### Checked and found sound (area 5)

`uniqueOutputs` and case-only differences; dedupe across doors (direct drop vs folder walk —
and note the walk returns `/private/var/…` where a drop returns `/var/…`, so
`standardizedFileURL` is load-bearing); cancel mid-run then Start again; **no `@Published`
property is mutated off the main actor** (compiled `-strict-concurrency=complete`, with a
control file proving the diagnostic fires when there is something to find); a file cannot
finish after `finishUp`; `log`/`stages` bounded.

## Area 6 — the four properties measured together (369 shipped-pipeline runs)

The first whole-corpus re-measurement of invariant 3 since the recent changes: **233/233
documents, 645 pages, 43,446 reference observations**, run in round-robin stratified order so
no prefix is biased (R37's lesson).

### A6.1 · Three of the four invariant-3 instruments are compromised, and the procedure is not executable

This is the finding that matters most here, because every number the register quotes about
the text layer comes through these.

- **`score-line-separation.swift` can move opposite to the property it names.** It divides
  PDFKit *lines* by Vision *fragments*, and Vision emits 1.0–8.5 fragments per visual line on
  this corpus, so it reads **35%–2533%**. On one document it reads 87% → 87% across a change
  from *no runaway line at all* to a **2,139-character** one; on another it reads 60% → 68%
  (improving) while the runaway-character share goes 10.2% → 30.0% (worsening). A third reads
  37% with **0 of 76 pairs merged**. Its level is meaningless and its direction unreliable —
  and `HANDOFF.md`'s "modern print keeps 100%, 1920s-50s 87-93%" comes from it.
- **`probe-line-edges.swift` is not an independent instrument** — behaviourally identical to
  `score-corpus`'s `start=`/`end=` columns on 48 of 48 documents. Invariant 3 lists four
  instruments; two of them are one instrument.
- **`probe-text-offset.swift`'s printed range is 1.7% artifact** — it scans upward and takes
  the first `contains(firstWord)` hit, so it locks onto a common word on the line *below*
  (`the` alone accounts for 33 of 87 outliers). The median is sound.
- **`score-corpus` prints `OK` when it measured nothing**: one document yields
  `start=-% end=-% off=nan words=-%` with an `OK` verdict, from a text layer holding 1,320
  words.
- **Invariant 3's re-measurement procedure cannot be run as written.** `probe-line-edges` and
  `probe-text-offset` both take a JSON of observations as argv[3], and **nothing in `Tools/`
  produces that file any more** — the helper writes a different shape. The agent had to write
  a producer to feed them.

### A6.3 · The constant-vs-property tension map, which did not exist before

- **`headroomFactor` has opposite optima on different material.** 1.5 removes every runaway

## Area 7 — `Flattener.swift`, numerical and concurrency lens (second pass)

### A7.1 · `mrcLayers` traps uncatchably on a document-declared page box, on the default route. VERIFIED — reachable crash

`sauvolaMask(…, window: max(Int(dpi / 4), 3))` — a bare `Int(Double)` on a number descending
entirely from what the file declares, 1,300 lines below the `safeInt` that exists for exactly
this. `MediaBox [0 0 1e-14 1.3e-14]` with an image declaring 8000×10400:

```
dpi = 5.76e+19    wide = 8000  high = 10400   product 83.2 MP
flatten gate (<=400MP): true    mrc gate (<=100MP): true
flatten OK -> published 68,950 bytes;  mrcLayers -> EXIT 133 (SIGTRAP)
```

`Int(1.44e19)` against `Int.max` 9.22e18. **Both gates pass because `wide` reduces
algebraically to the declared `/Width`**, so any raster size is reachable at any box scale,
and the page renders as an ordinary 8000×10400 sheet with visible ink that `qpdf --check`
calls clean. Verified end to end on shipped defaults (rebuild on, jbig2/qpdf present,
Automatic, one recognised word) with a tone wedge to route the page to the picture path.
R24's recorded harm verbatim: uncatchable, takes every concurrent file with it.

**This is the sibling A3.2's §4b sweep missed** — same function, four lines from the same
guard, and unlike A3.2 it needs nothing from Vision. The whole family is one grep: five
`Int(Double)` sites, two guarded, one is A3.2, one is this. Also worth an upper clamp: at a
merely large DPI the window computes to 3.6e12, so `r` covers the whole image and Sauvola
silently degenerates into the single global threshold its own doc comment exists to avoid.

### A7.2 · `inkCoverage` is the one fraction whose numerator and denominator are different populations. VERIFIED

It walks all of `grey` and divides by `width * height`. Every sibling takes both halves from
one population. Told a 4,000-pixel buffer is 20×20, it returns **10.0** (a coverage above 1);
told a 100-pixel buffer is 20×20, it under-reports 4x. No shipped caller mismatches, so it is
latent — but it is the one place a future caller or a tool handed a downsampled buffer would
silently rescale `pictureInkThreshold` and `pictureInkMinimumTone`, the two constants whose
miscalibration destroyed content twice.

### A7.3 · Smaller, from the numerical lens

- **Five more `Int`-product-inside-its-own-guard sites** (R24/R29's shape), including in
  `saturation` — R29's own function, in the arithmetic R29 was about — and `textRegionMask`
  allocating `[Bool](count: w * h)` on the line *before* `guard w > 0, h > 0`. All bounded
  today; recorded because §4b's rule is to name every sibling. `bilevelImage` also lacks the
  `grey.count` guard that `greyPNG` and `jpegRGB` have (A3.4's relative).
- **Otsu's partition and the renderer disagree by one bin**: Otsu means the dark class is
  `[0…t]`, `bilevelImage` renders `>= t` as white. **0 routing flips over 180 real pages**, so
  nothing is wrong — but the tone delta it generates is **58% of `pictureInkMinimumTone`**, so
  R38's constant sits inside this noise and that is worth knowing before it is re-tuned.
- **The `[90, 230]` clamp is the common case, not the edge case**: raw Otsu is below 90 on
  **56 of 180 sampled pages (31%)**. So "derived from its own histogram" is untrue for a third
  of the corpus, and moving the 90 moves a third of pages at once. The band is asymmetric at
  the top clamp but nothing is lost, and no corpus page clamps high.
- A3.4's `downsample` stretch figure is the **best** case: the bound is `(w mod f)/w`, so at
  f=16 on a 600 px page it is up to **2.5%**, measured 5.88% at a small size.

### Checked and found sound (area 7)

**`otsuThreshold` is correct** — verified against an independent reference written from the
definition with exact integer sums, sharing no rounding path: **0 mismatches on 120 real pages
and 13 adversarial histograms**, and **no overflow at 100 MP with real 100,000,000-element
buffers**. **`sauvolaMask`'s integral images are exact** — 0 differing pixels against a
brute-force per-window reference across six sizes and six flat images where the variance is a
cancellation of equal quantities. **`flatten`'s and `mrcLayers`' pixel dimensions cannot
diverge** — 288 combinations of awkward boxes, non-terminating DPIs and all four rotations, 0
divergences, which matters because `JBIG2.assemble` declares the stencil at `flatten`'s
numbers while the bitmap inside it is `mrcLayers`'. **Concurrency: nothing found**, measured
rather than argued — 10 real files × 3 rounds × 8 threads running `flatten` *and* `mrcLayers`,
0 differences against serial; the three encoders byte-identical across 8 threads × 480
buffers. **`largestImage` is now run rather than reasoned** — 34 hand-written PDFs including a
self-referential `/Resources`, a two-form cycle, and a dictionary reachable at two depths in
both key orderings; R25's memoisation still works. `safeInt`, `downsample`, `fillHoles`,
`inkOutsideText` and `textRegionMask` all match references, and no division by zero is
reachable.
