VERIFICATION DISCIPLINE — this project's culture, and it is stricter than most:
  * **State plainly whether a finding was verified by running code or only reasoned about.** Never report a
    change as working without the suite passing. "An entry without evidence is a rumour."
  * **When a measurement is surprising, suspect the instrument first.** Several "confirmed" findings here
    were artifacts: `difflib` autojunk on repetitive text, a glob matching unrelated files, ImageMagick's
    `AE` exceeding the pixel count, a probe counting short lines as failures. Ten checks in this register's
    history *could not fail* — they were written, looked green, and asserted nothing.
  * Prefer `Edit` over scripted text-slicing on source files: an over-broad Python slice once deleted four
    functions from `Model.swift`.
  * Do not invent numbers. If you did not measure it, say so.

  reinstates it. The things worth handing off:

      and it keeps paying: on 2026-08-15 it found 15 defects in a commit that was already "done", including

  ⚠️ **Tell every subagent NOT to run the suite** (`./run_tests.sh`, `./build.sh`, `Tools/mutate.py`). Two
  suites at once corrupt both, and a subagent is the easiest way to start a second one by accident.
  The one real limit is arithmetic, not policy: you have **one $20 session budget and your subagents spend
  out of it**, so aim them at work that actually needs doing rather than at speculative breadth. That is a
  budget fact to plan around, NOT a reason to do it all yourself — an agent that saves you 30k of context
  or catches one defect has already paid for itself.
  Your OWN model and effort are fixed before you start and you cannot change them mid-session. Each
  SUBAGENT's is yours to choose, per task. `Workflow`'s `agent()` takes `{effort, model}` per call and is the

  SPEND UP on judgment under irreversibility: the adversarial self-review of your own diff, anything touching
